On a fictional dataset, I asked Cortex Analyst for the June conversion rate. It said 85%. The right answer was 8%.

The semantic view exposes two dates and does not say which one defines a month for this metric. Cortex picked both. A written expected answer is what catches that.

One run, one semantic view, fictional data.

Stack: Snowflake semantic view, Cortex Analyst, dbt tests. 10 questions, 10 request IDs kept for audit.

How the check works

  1. 1

    What was asked

    10 business questions, the kind a head of growth or a finance lead asks every week. Each has an expected answer re-derived from the raw tables and a named owner (3 owners: finance lead, head of growth, product lead).

  2. 2

    How it is checked

    Each Cortex Analyst answer is compared with the expected answer. Pass: it matches and the metric is defined in the semantic view. Warning: the number may be right, but it was produced in a way you should not trust. Fail: wrong, missing or invented.

  3. 3

    What failed and why

    2 of 10 fail and 3 carry a warning. Every row below states the reason and keeps the request ID, so the exact Cortex call can be found again.

The lead finding: Q04, the June conversion rate

Cortex answered
85.26%
Expected
8.09%

Cortex filtered the date twice: once on sessions.session_date and again on bookings.booking_date. The second filter keeps only the sessions that have a June booking, so the denominator drops and the rate inflates.

81 confirmed bookings ÷ 95 sessions that have a June booking (including 14 sessions whose booking was cancelled) = 85.26%. The right denominator is all 1,001 June sessions: 81 ÷ 1,001 = 8.09%.

SQL generated by Cortex Analyst request 2f271fe5…d4f6

SELECT * FROM SEMANTIC_VIEW(
  MISSION_OS_DB.TRUST_DEMO_DEV.TRUST_DEMO_SV
  METRICS CONVERSION_RATE
  WHERE sessions.session_date >= '2026-06-01'
    AND sessions.session_date < '2026-07-01'
    AND bookings.booking_date >= '2026-06-01'
    AND bookings.booking_date < '2026-07-01'
)

session filter keeps June sessions. booking filter is the extra one.

Same metric with the session-date filter only

SELECT * FROM SEMANTIC_VIEW(
  MISSION_OS_DB.TRUST_DEMO_DEV.TRUST_DEMO_SV
  METRICS CONVERSION_RATE
  WHERE sessions.session_date >= '2026-06-01'
    AND sessions.session_date < '2026-07-01'
)

8.09% matches the expected answer 8.09%. Recomputed from the seed data in the dbt build.
